## Reading status and evidence

Status belongs to a statement on a specified domain, not permanently to a word.

| Code | Meaning in this glossary |
|---|---|
| D | Adopted definition or notation. |
| P | A finite proof/result is stated in the cited source, or an elementary consequence is explicitly scoped to its premises. |
| X | An explicit map or crosswalk is stated on the named carriers. Its evidence basis is still listed. |
| C | A conditional construction, exact only after its stated inputs and choices are supplied. |
| H | A hypothesis, candidate, or open native-construction obligation. |
| F | Physical or operational correspondence remains open. |
| R | A rejected shortcut or nonidentification. This does not mean all conceivable richer bridges are impossible. |

The evidence label is a separate axis. A retained report with matrices, a manuscript theorem, a retrieved chat statement, and a fresh computation are not interchangeable. **This update performs editorial and file-integrity checks, not a new scientific audit.** Most importantly, the latest full history/analyzer action and cocycle claims are preserved as conversation-reported advances pending their producer/action-table archive; their algebraic implications are not discarded.

A source pointer at each entry identifies where its wording, formulas, or boundaries come from. The source register also says whether the basis was a complete file, selected excerpts, or recovered related-chat material. Dedupe never upgrades evidence by counting repeated statements as independent support.

# I. Identity, scope, and native objects

<a id="term-quantum-relativity"></a>
## QR-001 - Quantum Relativity (QR)

**Aliases:** `QR`, `transducer theory`  
**Status:** D; H for the proposed physical theory  
**Source basis:** [BASE](#src-base); [D6](#src-d6); [SHAPE](#src-shape)

Quantum Relativity is a theory of relational complexity governed by conservation of information. It studies registered relational organizations and the admissible transductions through which their distinctions propagate, remain retained, become comparable, and acquire history. "Quantum Relativity is transducer theory" remains the compact operational description, not a competing definition.

The subject is relational complexity; the constraint is complete information accounting; the mechanism is admissible transduction. The quantum and geometric readings are proposed reductions of this architecture, not two already-proved physical theories joined merely by terminology. QR does not require a wavefunction or spacetime manifold as its starting ontology, but can use either in a declared mathematical representation.

This definition names a research program. It does not make every proposed complexity functional, source law, instrument, or physical correspondence an established consequence of the Thalion. See [Relational complexity](#term-relational-complexity), [Thalean conservation of information](#term-information-conservation), and [Quantum/geometric readings and the QR unification criterion](#term-unification).

## QR-002 - Thalean

**Aliases:** `Thalean naming`  
**Status:** D; historical naming rationale  
**Source basis:** [D6](#src-d6); [SHAPE](#src-shape)

"Thalean" names the program's commitment to explanation through natural principle rather than caprice, with Thales of Miletus and "everything is water" retained as the author's historical naming background. Water is not thereby asserted to be the literal substrate of QR, and the name is not an argument for the equations.

The secondary Tolkien resonance associated with "Thalion" is an authorial mnemonic for steadfastness or informational fidelity. It is neither the mathematical derivation of the object nor evidence for a physical correspondence. These naming notes belong to the program's introduction; they do not add operators, degrees of freedom, or empirical laws.

The operative use of the adjective is domain-specific: a Thalean statement concerns a declared object, action, construction, or hypothesis in this program. "Thalean" is not itself a proof-status label.

## QR-003 - Thalean graph

**Aliases:** `Thalean graph-level organism`  
**Status:** D; inherited carrier identification  
**Source basis:** [BASE](#src-base); [D6](#src-d6); [CHAT](#src-chat)

The Thalean graph is the full graph-level organism or object-class under discussion, centrally represented in this program by AT4val[60,6]. The name must not drift to a quotient, matrix, or visual rendering simply because that shadow is easier to display.

A graph designation, a concrete structured instance, and a functional description have different roles. AT4val[60,6] is the graph designation; Thalion is the object name; Thalean Transducer is the proposed functional name. These names can refer to the same native object while emphasizing different aspects of it.

The G15 sector system, M_sec, Q_sec, and its cubic overlap law are downstream legibility layers. They do not replace the full G60 object or automatically retain its signed-lift, transport, closure, and registration information. See [Thalion / one thalion / Thalean Transducer](#term-thalion) and [Quotient-visible shadow and compressed legibility](#term-quotient-shadow).

## QR-004 - Thalion / one thalion / Thalean Transducer

**Aliases:** `Thalion`, `1 thalion`, `one thalion`, `Thalean Transducer`  
**Status:** D; native properties retain their individual source statuses  
**Source basis:** [BASE](#src-base); [D6](#src-d6); [CHAT](#src-chat)

A thalion is one concrete functional cluster-instance of the G60 form AT4val[60,6], including its full relevant relationships and properties. Thalean Transducer is the functional name for that object considered through what its admitted operations distinguish, propagate, retain, compare, close, and register.

The statement that "G60 is the carrier" is a way to separate bare graph data from the additional structures being used in an argument. It is not a redefinition of a thalion as G15, a selected eigenspace, one vertex, or a freely decorated unrelated graph. Only explicitly supplied or derived structures belong to the admitted construction; proposed apparatus choices and physical readings remain separately marked.

"One thalion" is an instance-level unit, not a universal scalar of complexity, action, time, or information. Its finite state set does not imply that every possible ordered history of repeated use is finite in length. See [Finite carrier and extensible history](#term-extensible-history).

<a id="term-qr-transducer"></a>
## QR-005 - QR transducer

**Aliases:** `relational transducer`  
**Status:** D  
**Source basis:** [BASE](#src-base); [D6](#src-d6)

A QR transducer is a local relational system with a declared input domain, admission constraints, input-output operation, retained register, and any handoff interface required by its task. It identifies which continuations are admissible and realizes an admitted transformation; this does not mean that bare geometry already determines a unique next outcome.

The complete-input notation is:

```text
T_e : (x,m) -> (p,m')
```

Here x is incoming payload, m is prior local memory, p is propagated or onward-eligible output, and m' is the complete retained output. The abbreviation x -> (p,r) is safe only when x already includes the complete input and r includes all retained output needed for recovery.

A generic QR transducer need not be identified with the particular G60 Thalion. A native Thalean realization must provide the relevant carrier, admission rule, recovery map, and interface rather than merely adopt this input-output shape.

<a id="term-history-bearing-transducer"></a>
## QR-006 - History-bearing transducer

**Aliases:** No separate aliases.  
**Status:** D; mechanism-specific C/H  
**Source basis:** [BASE](#src-base); [D6](#src-d6)

A history-bearing transducer is one whose future admitted response depends on retained distinctions produced by earlier events. It is more than a finite machine accompanied by a passive external log: some retained distinction must actually enter the future admission or response law.

The local carrier can remain finite while an extensible register stores a growing history. But when that register affects the controller, it is part of the complete dynamical state; the total effective state is then not confined to the fixed finite carrier alone. This clarifies the baseline's finite-carrier/extensible-history language without claiming unbounded memory is contained inside finitely many vertices.

The operational test is [Informative coupling](#term-informative-coupling) or [Predictive history equivalence](#term-predictive-history): hold the other relevant context fixed and show a difference in available future tests or registered responses. A stored distinction that never participates in such a test is archival evidence, not established dynamical feedback.

<a id="term-native-admitted"></a>
## QR-007 - Native, admitted, and supplied structure

**Aliases:** `native`, `admitted`, `admission`  
**Status:** D; provenance discipline  
**Source basis:** [BASE](#src-base); [D6](#src-d6); [EPR](#src-epr)

"Native" means construction-derived from the declared source object and permitted operations, with the dependencies needed to reconstruct that derivation. "Admitted" means included in a specified grammar or contract. "Supplied" means taken as input. These roles can overlap, but they are not synonyms.

An algebraically definable operator is not automatically an admitted event. An admitted apparatus hypothesis is not automatically selected uniquely by the graph. A supplied representation can support an exact conditional theorem while leaving its native origin open. Each entry must identify which level is being used.

The independence requirement is crucial: a source, weight, or selection law cannot be called derived merely because it was chosen to reproduce the target Born table or another desired answer. Provenance records what constructs the object before the target comparison, as well as what was tested afterward.

<a id="term-carrier"></a>
## QR-008 - Carrier and representation

**Aliases:** `state carrier`, `linearized carrier`  
**Status:** D  
**Source basis:** [BASE](#src-base); [D6](#src-d6); [CHAT](#src-chat)

A carrier is the specified domain on which an action, state, relation, or readout is defined. It may be a finite set, graph, quotient, groupoid, history language, vector space, or operator module. A statement is incomplete when it names an operator but leaves its carrier unspecified.

For a finite set X, the linearized carrier R^X has a basis indexed by X. Its dimension equals |X|, but arbitrary subspaces of R^X are not automatically subsets of X. In particular a 7,200-dimensional harmonic subspace is not a demonstrated 7,200-element receipt set.

A representation encodes an admitted structure on another carrier. Faithfulness, preservation of products, preservation of inner products, and compatibility with readout are distinct properties. An isomorphism of abstract groups does not identify their concrete state actions. See [Intertwiner and exact crosswalk](#term-intertwiner) and the symbol register.

<a id="term-quotient-shadow"></a>
## QR-009 - Quotient-visible shadow and compressed legibility

**Aliases:** `shadow`, `compressed legibility`, `quotient-visible observable`  
**Status:** D  
**Source basis:** [BASE](#src-base); [D6](#src-d6); [CHAT](#src-chat)

A quotient-visible shadow is information retained by a specified projection or compression of a richer relational object. It is useful precisely because it forgets distinctions that the intended readout does not need. It should not be treated as a complete reconstruction unless sufficiency has been proved.

Examples include G60 -> G30 -> G15, the sector incidence M_sec, and Q_sec = M_sec M_sec^T. A scalar, Gram matrix, or projective axis can preserve an invariant while erasing lift orientation, phase, incidence, or order of execution.

Apparent loss under projection is compatible with information conservation of the complete event. The missing distinction must remain in another output or retained register if recovery requires it. Equal quotient values therefore do not imply equal complete states, equal histories, or equal future responses.

<a id="term-context"></a>
## QR-010 - Complete comparison context

**Aliases:** `context`, `registration context`  
**Status:** D  
**Source basis:** [BASE](#src-base); [D6](#src-d6); [FREEDOM](#src-freedom)

A comparison context is the collection of conditions held fixed when admissibility, response, freedom, or informativeness is evaluated. It includes the relevant current state, retained variables, preparation, apparatus setting, reference presentation, and allowed test family. "Complete" is relative to that declared test, not a claim to know the whole universe.

Changing a passive reference can change coordinates without changing the underlying admitted relation. Changing an apparatus constraint can change the relation itself. Those two operations must not be hidden under the same word "context."

Expressions such as Adm(z), P(x | Ctx,Ref), and O_(z,r)(w) should therefore name what is fixed and what is varied. This is especially important for the retained-history actuator, freedom carry-forward, and contextual EPR readouts.

# II. Possibility, action, events, and historical registration

<a id="term-relation"></a>
## QR-011 - Relation

**Aliases:** `available relational possibility`  
**Status:** D  
**Source basis:** [BASE](#src-base); [D6](#src-d6)

A relation is a locally available connection between distinguishable states, interfaces, or transducers through which an admissible event could occur. Adjacency is one concrete representation; a typed interaction interface is another. The compact reading is "relation = available possibility."

Availability is not transmission. A carrier edge can show that an interaction is possible in principle while a context-dependent admission domain determines whether the present input can use it. Relation therefore precedes realized action without being an unrestricted promise that every imaginable transformation is allowed.

The definition is used operationally: identify the endpoints, the interface or incidence, and the relevant admission conditions. The richer philosophical claim that objects are persistent relational organizations belongs to the QR interpretation, not a substitute for supplying those data in a finite construction.

## QR-012 - Possibility, actuality, and impossibility

**Aliases:** `possibility`, `actuality`, `impossible continuation`, `unchosen possibility`  
**Status:** D  
**Source basis:** [BASE](#src-base); [CHAT](#src-chat); [BORNCHAT](#src-bornchat)

A possibility is a continuation compatible with the current declared constraints. An actuality is a realized event. An impossible continuation is excluded by those constraints. "Did not occur" is not equivalent to "was impossible before the event."

The double-slit discussion fixes the realized occurrence as singular while retaining a plural family of admissible alternatives before registration. This is the adopted QR reading of actuality; it does not by itself derive an interference experiment or a unique outcome-selection mechanism.

A counterfactual must retain its time and context. An alternative that was permitted at one prefix can cease to be a possible extension of the realized history afterward. That does not retroactively make its earlier permission false. See [Foreclosed alternative](#term-foreclosure) and [Finite-frame continuation family](#term-continuation-family).

**Source basis:** [BASE](#src-base); [CHAT](#src-chat); [FREEDOM](#src-freedom); [D6](#src-d6)

Admissibility is satisfaction of the declared native relational constraints for a continuation or transduction. Permissible action denotes the allowed action family, including the structure that distinguishes and relates its alternatives. It need not be a scalar probability distribution.

For a specified context z, write Adm(z) for the family of admitted elementary continuations. Additional registration or retained history can refine this family, but a passive renaming of a reference must not manufacture permission. Recoverability and handoff conditions remain separate explicit requirements where they are part of the event contract.

Admissibility is not designation or realization. The finite routing example gives one forbidden and two lawful continuations at each relevant directed quotient edge. ## QR-014 - Continuation

**Status:** D

A continuation is a lawful possible next relation or admitted elementary operation available from the present relational and registration context. It remains a possibility until it is realized.

## QR-015 - Designation and selection

**Aliases:** `selection`, `chooser`  
**Status:** D; native selector remains mechanism-specific  
**Source basis:** [BASE](#src-base); [CHAT](#src-chat); [BORNCHAT](#src-bornchat)

Designation specifies which member of an admissible family is selected, named, or assigned for an execution. When designation means a dynamical choice rather than a label convention, the actual selecting mechanism must be supplied. Naming a branch b rather than ab is not the same as causing it to occur.

The canonical separation is:

```text
permission != designation != realization != registration
```

Geometry can determine a lawful pair without choosing a member. A retained variable can influence the choice without proving a complete autonomous source law. A receipt can identify the member that occurred without having selected it upstream.

This distinction prevents a fitted scheduler, a chosen orientation section, or a post hoc history classification from being silently promoted into the law of becoming. The glossary records the source-law frontier rather than filling it by definition.

**Status:** D

Informative action is an admissible realized continuation that creates or changes a registered relational distinction. The phrase "action is always real" concerns realized action. An admissible action type or member of Adm(z) can be discussed before it is realized; that use names a possible operation. Keeping the type/occurrence distinction resolves the otherwise circular phrase "set of realized actions available before realization."

## QR-017 - Admissible transduction

**Aliases:** No separate aliases.  
**Status:** D; P for injectivity/recovery equivalence on a declared finite domain

An admissible transduction is a local transformation of a complete incoming relational state that satisfies the declared constraints and preserves the information needed for reconstruction from its complete output. It reorganizes information between onward output and retained output rather than authorizing unaccounted erasure.

```text
T_e : D_e -> P_e x R_e
Rec_e o T_e = id_(D_e)
```

**Status:** D  
**Source basis:** [BASE](#src-base); [D6](#src-d6)

The propagated component is the output eligible to acquire an adjacent field address under the admitted handoff rule. It is what continues across that interface, not necessarily everything that was present in the incoming state.

Recovery belongs to the complete propagated-plus-retained output. The propagated component may therefore be insufficient by itself: two distinct inputs can have the same onward component while their receipts distinguish them. The phrase "the propagated component moves" describes this interface role. It does not establish that a scalar complexity value, electric charge, energy, or a physical particle has moved unless the relevant encoding, transport, and decoding are separately supplied.

A retained receipt is a complementary distinction required, together with the propagated component and relevant retained memory, to account for a completed admissible transduction or handoff. It is part of the complete output, not waste and not a license to discard the input distinction.

"Retained" identifies its accounting role. Depending on the construction, the receipt can remain local, be encoded in a carrier, or later be transported. **Source basis:** [BASE](#src-base); [D6](#src-d6); [CHAT](#src-chat)

Registration is the admitted process by which a realized relational distinction is represented in a receipt, pointer, stable record, or history domain. It answers what was recorded as having happened. It is not automatically the upstream process that selected the event.

A faithful registration preserves the intended distinction or measure at the interface it is registering. A new operation that changes the measure, introduces new information, or changes the preparation is a different process and needs its own accounting. Registration can be physically interactive; calling it downstream does not mean it is causally inert.

Event primacy is the QR principle that becoming is modeled through completed relational occurrences and their composition, while states represent conditions before, after, or across those occurrences. The compact formulation is "physical becoming is fundamentally event composition, not merely state succession."

**Status:** D

The order matters. Histories with the same endpoint can differ in their retained receipts, orientation, or later admissible response. Becoming therefore is not defined merely as a scalar increase, a rise in complexity, or a count of unexplained updates.

Registered history is the ordered compositional record of realized relational events together with the retained distinctions that keep those occurrences accountable. "History = attested actuality" is its compact reading. It is richer than a list of visible endpoint configurations.

An extensible log acquires another occurrence when a new event is appended. This statement does not imply that every compressed history observable grows, that every event creates an independent degree, or that tau_Th increases at every step. The full word, a retained sufficient register, and a scalar history readout are different objects.

In the QR framing, a law is a stable constraint or invariant of admissible relational composition. "Relation is primitive to law" means that the program starts with relations and asks what transformations they admit, rather than imagining an external command applied to otherwise independent objects.

A foreclosed alternative is a continuation that was admitted at an earlier prefix but is no longer a compatible extension of the now-realized history in the same declared frame. This gives a precise reading of the discussion's claim that an unrealized alternative becomes impossible after an action: it becomes incompatible with that realized occurrence as its alternative past, not impossible in every future context.

An opportunity can survive even when its earlier ordering does not. Choosing one task now can foreclose the history "the other task first" while leaving the other task available later. Thus historical alternatives and carried-forward action capacity are not the same inventory.

<a id="term-information-conservation"></a>
## QR-030 - Thalean conservation of information

**Aliases:** `event-local conservation of relational information`, `conservation of information`  
**Status:** D/H as QR principle; P for declared recoverable maps  
**Source basis:** [BASE](#src-base); [D6](#src-d6); [FREEDOM](#src-freedom)

Thalean conservation of information says that admissible becoming may redistribute relational information between propagation and receipt but may not erase the distinction structure needed to reconstruct the completed event. Its primitive mathematical content is recoverability of the complete input from the complete admitted output.

The slogan "what propagates plus what is retained accounts for the event" is not an already-derived additive entropy equation. A scalar information functional would need its own definition and, where relevant, correlation terms. Information conservation does not imply that complexity, local freedom, every marginal, or every visible readout is numerically constant.

Today's keeper is "freedom can be spent; information cannot be discarded." Future choices can be restricted while the output still records the distinction needed to account for that restriction. This is a structural accounting requirement, not yet an identification with every standard physical conservation law.

<a id="term-recoverability"></a>
## QR-031 - Recoverability

**Aliases:** `reconstruction`, `reversible on the admitted image`  
**Status:** D/P  
**Source basis:** [BASE](#src-base); [D6](#src-d6)

Recoverability means that a reconstruction map returns the complete input from the complete output on the admitted image. In a finite classical model this is equivalent to injectivity of the complete transduction.

```text
Rec_e(T_e(z)) = z
```

Either output alone can be many-to-one. For example, the supplied bit illustration T(u,v)=(u+v,v), with addition modulo two, is recovered by (p,r)->(p+r,r). The first component alone loses a distinction; the pair does not. This illustrates reorganization, not copying an extra input bit into existence.

Recovery on an image does not imply every formal output pair is reachable, every inverse is an admitted forward experiment, or every history word can be physically erased. The source domain, retained memory, and permitted inverse operation must be specified.

<a id="term-complete-input"></a>
## QR-032 - Complete input and complete microscopic description

**Aliases:** `complete input`, `prior memory`, `complete microscopic state`  
**Status:** D  
**Source basis:** [D6](#src-d6); [BASE](#src-base)

The complete input contains every variable whose distinction must survive the stated transduction, including prior local memory. If the operation is T_e(x,m)=(p,m'), retaining only enough information to recover x is insufficient when distinct prior memories m were also part of the admitted domain.

This complete accounting description differs from the [Complete operational QR state](#term-operational-state). The latter can intentionally identify histories that no admitted future test distinguishes. Such a reduction is useful but must not be substituted into a microscopic recovery claim without saying which distinctions are being quotiented out.

The same distinction governs archives: a short glossary can be an excellent re-entry surface without being a lossless transcript. Its source ledger and preserved baseline make the compression accountable; they do not turn the glossary into the complete historical record.

<a id="term-receipt-capacity"></a>
## QR-033 - Receipt capacity

**Aliases:** `minimum receipt size`  
**Status:** P under a declared finite classical model  
**Source basis:** [D6](#src-d6)

Receipt capacity is the amount of retained label space needed to make a prescribed propagated map recoverable. For a finite map p:X->Y completed by a receipt r:X->R, the pair (p,r) is injective exactly when r distinguishes every pair of inputs lying in the same p-fiber.

The manuscript's finite bound is:

```text
|R| >= max_y |p^-1(y)|
```

With freely assignable receipt labels this bound is attainable. It counts distinguishable labels, not a thermodynamic energy or an independently calibrated physical information unit. A bit count would require a chosen encoding and a ceiling logarithm.

This term sharpens "complementary receipt": the needed record is relative to what the propagated component forgets. When p is injective, one constant receipt label is enough for reconstruction, though an operational apparatus may retain more.

## QR-034 - Complete operational QR state

**Aliases:** `complete QR state`, `effective QR state`  
**Status:** D  
**Source basis:** [BASE](#src-base); [D6](#src-d6)

The complete operational QR state is the present configuration together with retained distinctions relevant to future admitted response. Its completeness is relative to a declared apparatus, continuation language, and response family, not to every microscopic detail of the past.

A retained distinction matters when it changes which future tests are admitted or how an admitted test responds. Two equal visible endpoints can therefore be operationally different. Conversely an inaccessible detail not represented in any admitted future test need not be retained in the effective state description.

This is not a permission to destroy distinctions in a complete information-conserving transduction. It is a choice of reduced description. ## QR-035 - Predictive history equivalence

**Aliases:** `predictive history`, `predictive history class`  
**Status:** D/P on a declared response language  
**Source basis:** [BASE](#src-base); [D6](#src-d6)

Two histories are predictively equivalent when they have the same admitted future language and the same registered response to every test in that language. Both conditions are needed.

```text
h1 ~_pred h2 iff
  L(h1) = L(h2), and
  O(h1,w) = O(h2,w) for every w in that common language.
```

The baseline's wording in terms of "common future continuations" is tightened using the later manuscript. Equality only on the intersection of two different languages can be vacuous, even when one history permits actions the other forbids. That is not predictive equivalence.

A predictive class is a state-reduction tool. It does not imply that the full records are identical or that all forgotten information has been erased physically. Where the continuation grammar is closed under common extension, the equivalence must remain compatible with that extension.

<a id="term-memory"></a>
## QR-036 - Memory

**Aliases:** `state-relevant retained history`  
**Status:** D  
**Source basis:** [BASE](#src-base); [D6](#src-d6)

Memory is retained distinction with future discriminating power in a specified transducer or apparatus. The test is operational: some future admission decision or response can depend on the distinction under a controlled comparison.

A receipt can exist as historical evidence without the next operation reading it. Conversely a compact retained register can preserve exactly the distinctions needed for future response without storing a verbatim transcript. Memory, receipt, archive, and complete history are therefore related but not interchangeable.

The physical interpretation of a particular memory carrier needs an implementation. The finite definition neither imports consciousness nor assigns arbitrary inaccessible past details to physical state. It supplies the criterion used when deciding whether an alleged hidden history actually does explanatory work in the model.

<a id="term-return"></a>
## QR-037 - Visible return and complete return

**Aliases:** `visible return`, `complete return`, `state recurrence`, `history recurrence`, `present state and retained history`  
**Status:** D/P on stated projections  
**Source basis:** [BASE](#src-base); [D6](#src-d6); [G9000](#src-g9000)

Visible return occurs when a chosen projected or reduced configuration returns to a previous value. Complete return requires recurrence of the complete state on the declared carrier. They are not equivalent when a projection forgets lift or retained-register information.

The registered five-frame example distinguishes q(F^10 x)=q(x) from F^10 x=x: the G900 receipt can repeat while the G1800 lift differs. Full carrier recurrence at F^20 still does not make an append-only history word empty; a new traversal has occurred even if the finite state recurs.

Thus three levels must be named: projected-state return, complete finite-carrier return, and equality of an ordered historical record. The baseline's phrase "history returns" must always specify a compressed history state or equivalence, rather than imply that chronology has automatically been undone.

## QR-038 - Closure

**Aliases:** `relational closure`  
**Status:** D; operation-specific C/H  
**Source basis:** [BASE](#src-base); [D6](#src-d6)

Closure is an admitted operation bringing relational histories or presentations into a common comparison context so that a distinction can be registered or tested. It need not erase the difference between the compared histories or restore their complete initial state.

A graph cycle, a quotient return, an episode closure, and a comparison closure can all participate in the program, but each has its own domain and criterion. A closed quotient path can retain nontrivial holonomy upstairs. A completed comparison can leave a residue that matters later.

The term does not assert that every thalion autonomously seeks closure. That would require a source or feedback law. Preserve the separation "closure != complete historical return" and distinguish the closure operation from its retained [Closure residue](#term-closure-residue).

**Status:** D  
**Source basis:** [BASE](#src-base); [D6](#src-d6)

A closure residue is the retained relational distinction that survives an admitted closure or comparison. It records what remains different after the histories have been brought into the required common context.

Its type can be a group element, parity, phase, vector, register value, or another specified object. No universal scalar or common central element is assigned to every residue. A residue contributes to a receipt when that retained content is part of the completed-event accounting.

The phrase "history enters rich; closure leaves a residue" describes a compression, not automatic destruction of the rest of the complete information. Whether the residue affects the future is the separate [Closure feedback](#term-closure-feedback) test. Closure feedback is a dependence of a later admitted continuation or registered response on a residue retained from an earlier closure. Writing Adm_(n+1)=Adm_(n+1)(r_n) names the proposed dependence; it does not prove it.

A witness must compare different r_n values while holding the other relevant context fixed. The residue might change permission, a branch designation, or the response to a common future test. Those mechanisms should be reported separately rather than compressed into the assertion that history "must" select the next event.

The existence of a residue, a storage register, or a nontrivial group commutator alone does not establish feedback. The current program contains candidate and scoped coupling examples, not a universal law that every retained distinction is read by every later event.

<a id="term-rsr"></a>
## QR-042 - Relational Spin Residue (RSR)

**Aliases:** `RSR`, `spin-sensitive closure residue`  
**Status:** D/C; F for physical spin correspondence  
**Source basis:** [BASE](#src-base); [D6](#src-d6); [CHAT](#src-chat)
