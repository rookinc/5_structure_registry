---

## Relation

A locally available adjacency between distinguishable states or transducers through which an event could occur.

A relation is not yet a transmission. It is an available relational possibility with enough structure to determine what may interact with what.

Short form:

relation = available possibility

---

## Admissible transduction

A local, reversible transformation of an incoming relational state that preserves the information required for a legitimate handoff.

The transducer separates what can propagate from what must remain locally recorded.

"Admissible" means that the transformation satisfies the native relational constraints rather than merely being algebraically possible.

Schematic form:

incoming relational state
-> propagated component + retained receipt

---

## Propagated component

The part of the transduced relational state that can be handed to an adjacent transducer while preserving admissibility.

It is the information that actually acquires the next field address.

The propagated component moves.

---

## Retained receipt

The complementary relational information that remains at the sending or encounter location because it is required to make the completed handoff reversible and attestable.

It is evidence of what happened locally, not discarded information.

The retained receipt stays.

When the retained quantity is specifically the spin-related central or projective residue, it is called the Relational Spin Residue, or RSR.

Important distinction:

Sigma = relational comparison or encounter operation/interface
RSR   = spin-related retained residue produced by that operation

---

## Adjacent handoff

The completed transfer of the propagated component across exactly one admissible neighboring relationship.

This is the primitive unit of field propagation.

Under the current Thalean normalization:

c_Th = 1

meaning:

one transmission per admissible adjacent relationship per event step

or equivalently:

one event per event

More formally, one irreducible event of becoming can actualize at most one new adjacent relationship.

Saturation occurs when one adjacent handoff is completed per event step.

---

## History

The ordered structure produced by completed relational events and their retained receipts.

History is not merely a sequence parameter attached from outside. It is the persistent record of which possibilities were actually transduced and handed off.

Short form:

history = attested actuality

---

## Event

An event is the admissible local transduction together with its completed adjacent handoff and retained receipt.

It is the primitive conversion:

available relational possibility
-> locally resolved action
-> propagated consequence + retained evidence

An event therefore creates both:

1. new address downstream
2. local evidence upstream

The succession of such certified events constitutes history.

---

## Compact reading

A relation presents a possibility.

An admissible transducer resolves that possibility into what may continue and what must be retained.

The continuing part crosses one neighboring relation.

The retained part certifies the event.

The succession of such certified events is history.

A Thalion is a finite structured relational transducer whose carrier is G60, identified with AT4val[60,6], together with only those quotient, deck, transport, closure, history, and readout structures that have been explicitly supplied or derived.

Thus:

    Thalion
    =
    finite carrier
    +
    admissible transducer structure

G60 alone is the carrier.

The full Thalion includes the admissible structures that determine what the carrier can distinguish, propagate, retain, close, and register.

## Thalean Transducer

A Thalean Transducer is a Thalion viewed through the admissible transformations it performs and the relational distinctions those transformations retain, propagate, erase, compare, or expose under projection.

Its primitive operation remains:

    incoming relational state
    -> admissible transduction
    -> propagated component + retained receipt

The Thalean graph supplies the finite carrier on which this transducer architecture is realized.

## Present State and Retained History

QR distinguishes present visible configuration from retained ordered history.

Thus:

    finite present state
    !=
    retained ordered history

A finite carrier may revisit the same visible state while retaining a different complete relational history.

Therefore:

    visible return
    !=
    complete return

and:

    quotient-visible identity
    does not imply
    identity of complete registered state

This is a general QR boundary, not merely a property of one quotient construction.

## Closure

Closure is the operation by which accumulated relational history is brought into a common comparison context so that a distinction can become registered.

Schematic form:

    lifted or oriented history
    -> closure or comparison
    -> registered observable

Closure does not necessarily restore the complete prior history.

Therefore:

    relational closure
    !=
    historical return

Two histories may become comparable or locally closed while still retaining a relational distinction between them.

---

## Closure Residue

A closure residue is the retained relational distinction left after histories have been brought into a common comparison or closure context.

Schematic form:

    rich history
    -> closure
    -> retained residue

The residue preserves whatever distinction remains relevant after closure.

Compactly:

    history enters rich

    closure leaves a residue

    the residue may constrain the future

A closure residue may function as part of a retained receipt when the event includes a comparison or closure operation.

No universal identification is presently made between every residue appearing in different QR or Thalean constructions.

## Closure Feedback

Closure feedback is the dependence of a later admissible continuation on a relational residue retained from an earlier closure.

If r_n is a retained closure residue, write:

    A_(n+1) = A_(n+1)(r_n)

where A_(n+1) denotes the admissible continuation set available at the next stage.

Thus:

    retained residue
    -> modified admissible future

Status:

    conditional working principle

The present theory does not claim that every Thalion autonomously seeks closure or that every retained residue participates in future dynamics.

Closure feedback is present only where such dependence is explicitly derived.

## Predictive History

A retained history is physically relevant when it has future discriminating power.

Two histories are predictively distinct when a common admissible future continuation produces different registered responses from them.

Thus:

    memory
    =
    retained distinction
    with future discriminating power

A possible predictive-history equivalence relation is:

    H1 ~ H2

only when every relevant common admissible future continuation produces the same registered response from H1 and H2.

This gives an operational criterion for which parts of retained history belong to the effective QR state.

Not every inaccessible detail of the past counts as physical memory.

## Transport Cocycle

A transport cocycle is an additive relational displacement assigned to composable admissible histories.

For admissible histories gamma_1 and gamma_2:

    Xi(gamma_2 o gamma_1)
    =
    Xi(gamma_2)
    +
    Xi(gamma_1)

where Xi records accumulated transport displacement.

A corresponding positive history cost may satisfy:

    T(gamma_2 o gamma_1)
    =
    T(gamma_2)
    +
    T(gamma_1)

with:

    T(gamma) > 0

for nontrivial forward histories.

A long-history propagation rate may then be written:

    v(gamma)
    =
    Xi(gamma)
    /
    T(gamma)

The current canonical Thalean causal definition c_Th = 1 is a more specific event-depth realization of this general transport architecture.

    a

    ab

Thus:

    V4

    three pairings of a four-state local orientation cell

This is a finite relational orientation structure.

It is not by itself a claim about literal Euclidean spatial axes.

## Sheet

A sheet is a distinguished relational, spectral, quotient, or deck presentation of the same finite carrier.

A sheet is not automatically:

    a physical surface
    an extra spatial dimension
    a literal Euclidean layer

Distinct sheets may expose different relational structure while referring to the same underlying carrier.

Therefore:

    spectral sheet
    !=
    physical sheet

unless an independent physical crosswalk is explicitly derived.

## Continuation Multiplicity

Continuation multiplicity is the number, weight, or dimension of distinct admissible future histories available from a present relational configuration over a specified event depth.

For a transition object M, one possible finite-depth count is:

    E_L(i)
    =
    (M^L 1)_i

where E_L(i) counts admissible length-L continuations from state i.

The definition is general.

A specific transition matrix, stochastic process, or source graph is not considered native merely because it realizes useful continuation counts.

## Continuation Entropy

Continuation entropy measures genuine variation or growth in distinct admissible future histories.

It must distinguish dynamical branching from multiplicity introduced only by regular covering structure.

Therefore:

    deck multiplicity
    !=
    dynamical multiplicity

A regular finite deck may duplicate available lifts without generating new information-bearing future alternatives.

Likewise:

    direct-sum structure
    does not imply
    hidden tensor-product branching

Continuation entropy should be attributed only to genuinely distinct admissible futures.

## History-Bearing Transducer

A history-bearing transducer is a finite relational transducer whose present response may depend on retained distinctions generated by prior admissible events.

Its architecture is:

    finite carrier
    +
    local orientation
    +
    admissible transduction
    +
    retained history

A history-bearing transducer therefore need not contain an unbounded instantaneous state space.

Its unboundedness, where present, may reside in extensible registered history rather than in the finite local carrier.

## Commonality Guardrails

Three distinctions should remain explicit.

First:

    visible return
    !=
    complete return

A visible or quotient state may recur while retained history differs.

Second:

    deck multiplicity
    !=
    dynamical multiplicity

A regular covering multiplicity does not by itself generate meaningful branching or entropy.

Third:

    carrier symmetry
    !=
    execution symmetry

A symmetry of the underlying finite carrier does not automatically survive in a particular event grammar, history process, or transduction rule.

These are structural no-go boundaries for future QR derivations.
