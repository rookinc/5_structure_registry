\section{What Counts as a QR Prediction?}\label{sec:predictions}
A structural consequence follows from stated finite hypotheses. A quantitative correspondence connects a native quantity to a physical observable and its units. A discriminating prediction fixes an observable relation that comparison theories do not already require. An exact identity or an admissible representation can support the first two levels without constituting the third.

\section{Finite-History Corrections to Quantum Statistics}\label{sec:finitecorrections}
A native chronological law could constrain sequential correlations or finite-count fluctuations. Independent Bernoulli trials have variance $Np(1-p)$; the floor sequence of Section~\ref{sec:frequency} has uniformly bounded count discrepancy. These share a limiting weight but are different processes. A prediction requires a derived source law and a stated preparation/resetting protocol, with instrumental memory, drift, and selection modeled.

\section{Propagation--Receipt Complementarity}\label{sec:receiptprediction}
The receipt-capacity bound is exact. A carrier-specific result would derive the propagation map, its minimal completing residue, or restrictions on its later use. A quantum implementation must state both its receipt observable and transmitted channel and satisfy Proposition~28.1. An arbitrary extra record is not a substitute for that interface.

\section{Recurrence--Causality Separation}\label{sec:recurrenceprediction}
Causal support, recurrence, and modal parity are distinct operator properties. The substantive target is a derived joint constraint: for example, an admitted support may permit only specified recurrence sectors. Such a restriction must follow from the native admission grammar, not from choosing a favorable spectral basis after the result is known.

\section{History-Conditioned Geometry}\label{sec:geometryprediction}
Compare preparations with matched visible variables but different operationally relevant histories, followed by the same admitted test. A response difference first establishes a memory effect. A geometric interpretation additionally requires a consistent metric or connection description across probes. Redundant logs, ordinary apparatus hysteresis, and changes of preparation must be controlled.
