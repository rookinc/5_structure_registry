### How to read the entries

Each concept has one home entry. **Aliases** are alternate names for that definition. **Indexed component terms** are distinct objects, properties, or related constructions explained together in one entry; they are not asserted to be synonyms. Lookup labels include editorial search aids as well as wording found in the sources. The **Standing** line distinguishes working definitions, source-recorded finite structures, conditional constructions, interpretations, and unresolved or merely reported claims. Source links use portable filenames, sections, and document hashes rather than requiring a chat interface.

Notation changes only disambiguate symbols; they do not assert new mathematical identities. Lowercase spectral "eigenmode" is distinct from capitalized registered-event **Mode**.

### Core organizing statements

Quantum Relativity studies **relational complexity under information-preserving transduction**. A relation makes a continuation available; informative action realizes a continuation; a completed propagation event includes the handoff and its complementary accounting; registered history retains composition.

Permission is not designation. A record is not automatically a selector. Measure, empirical frequency, and process provenance remain different. A selected reference supplies a presentation, not a missing native coupling.

The causal datum is `c_Th = 1`. Thalean time remains oriented registered-history winding. Freedom can be preserved, transformed, gained, or foreclosed without authorizing loss of the information required to account for an event.

## 01. Foundations and relational complexity

<a id="quantum-relativity"></a>

### TH-001. Quantum Relativity

**Aliases:** QR; Quantum Relativity as transducer theory.

**Standing:** Working definition.

A theory of relational complexity governed by conservation of information. QR studies registered relational objects and the admissible transductions that reorganize them while preserving the distinctions required for a complete account. Quantum and geometric descriptions are investigated as compatible reductions of this process.

The earlier wording, "QR is transducer theory," names the mechanism and remains useful shorthand; the relational-complexity formulation is the current thesis. It is a research framework, not a declaration that all of quantum mechanics or general relativity has been derived.

**Sources:** [CHAT-COMPLEX](#src-chat-complex), [D6](#src-d6), [CORE](#src-core). D6 Introduction and Appendix A; CORE Transducer-Theory Extension.

<a id="thalean"></a>

### TH-002. Thalean

**Standing:** Working definition.

The program's name for its natural-principle approach, referring to Thales of Miletus and the abandonment of capricious explanation. The historical water motif is naming background, not a claim that literal water is the mathematical or physical substrate. This is the adopted naming explanation, not an independent historical thesis.

**Sources:** [CHAT-COMPLEX](#src-chat-complex), [D6](#src-d6). Introduction; Appendix A.

<a id="thalean-graph"></a>

### TH-003. Thalean graph

**Standing:** Working definition.

The full graph-level object or object-class under discussion, with its declared relations and structure. Its central concrete carrier is the graph designated `AT4val[60,6]`. A claim about a quotient, a coordinate chart, or a readout is not automatically a claim about the full Thalean graph.

Use **Thalion** for one concrete G60 functional cluster-instance and **Thalean Transducer** for its functional name. The G15 sector system and its matrices are downstream legibility layers, not alternative names for the Thalion.

**Sources:** [D6](#src-d6), [CHAT-BORN](#src-chat-born). Sections 2-3; retained naming convention.

<a id="thalion"></a>

### TH-004. Thalion

**Aliases:** Thalean Transducer; 1 thalion.

**Standing:** Working definition.

One concrete structured G60 instance, designated `AT4val[60,6]`, considered with its full relationships and properties and with the quotient, deck, transport, closure, history, and readout structures explicitly supplied or derived for that instance. "One thalion" counts an instance, not a G15 sector or one vertex.

**Thalean Transducer** is the proposed functional name of this same object. It emphasizes what the instance can distinguish, propagate, retain, compare, and register. External apparatus choices and unproved physical correspondences remain separately declared.

**Sources:** [D6](#src-d6), [CORE](#src-core), [CHAT-BORN](#src-chat-born). Definition 3.1; Appendix A.

<a id="qr-transducer"></a>

### TH-005. QR transducer

**Aliases:** Relational transducer.

**Standing:** Working definition.

A relational system implementing a declared local transformation of a complete input into propagated and retained output. Its admitted map can be written

\[
\mathcal T_e:(x,m)\longmapsto(p,m'),
\]

where old memory \(m\) is included whenever it belongs to the input. Admission requires both the relational constraints and recovery of the complete input from the complete output. A transducer can expose a lawful continuation without supplying a uniquely determined outcome-selection law.

**Sources:** [D6](#src-d6), [CORE](#src-core). Sections 3 and 7.

<a id="registered-relational-object"></a>

### TH-006. Registered relational object

**Aliases:** Registered object.

**Standing:** Working definition.

A relational structure together with a specified registration interface, retained distinctions, context, and equivalence relation on presentations. It is the typed object \(R\) to which a complexity readout is applied.

A carrier state, a completed event with its receipt, and a history can each be registered objects, but they are different object types. Their notation must not silently substitute one for another.

**Sources:** [D6](#src-d6). Definition 4A.1.

<a id="complete-microscopic-description"></a>

### TH-007. Complete microscopic description

**Aliases:** Complete information-preserving description; Complete account.

**Standing:** Working definition.

The full declared input or output needed for information-preserving reconstruction, including prior memory and any environment or receipt variables used by the map. Completeness is relative to an explicitly stated accounting boundary.

This differs from the **complete operational state**, which retains distinctions relevant to the specified future tests. Operational irrelevance does not authorize erasing a variable from a microscopic recovery claim.

**Sources:** [D6](#src-d6). Sections 7, 11 and 15; Appendix A.

<a id="complete-operational-state"></a>

### TH-008. Complete operational state

**Aliases:** Complete QR state; Effective QR state.

**Standing:** Working definition.

The present relational configuration together with retained distinctions relevant to future admitted continuations and responses. Two visible configurations can agree while representing different complete operational states.

Conversely, two microscopic histories can belong to the same operational state when their admitted future languages and all specified responses agree. This is not a claim that arbitrary inaccessible details of the past affect present behavior.

**Sources:** [D6](#src-d6), [CORE](#src-core). Appendix A; predictive-history discussion.

<a id="registration-interface"></a>

### TH-009. Registration interface

**Aliases:** Registration boundary.

**Standing:** Working definition.

The specified means by which an object, event, or output becomes represented and comparable: its accessible variables, record format, readout rules, and retained context. Registration is part of the declared apparatus or mathematical construction, not an unexplained external observer operation.

A registration interface may be sufficient for attestation while insufficient for full reconstruction.

**Sources:** [D6](#src-d6). Sections 4A-4B, 9 and 26.

<a id="context"></a>

### TH-010. Context

**Aliases:** Relational context; Complete context.

**Standing:** Working definition.

The declared circumstances held fixed when defining admission, dependence, or readout. Context can include the current configuration, retained variables, selected references, apparatus constraints, and analyzer settings.

A comparison such as "changing this receipt changes the future" is meaningful only after stating which other variables and which common tests are fixed.

**Sources:** [D6](#src-d6). Definitions 4A.1, 5.1 and 6A.1.

<a id="registered-distinction"></a>

### TH-011. Registered distinction

**Aliases:** Relational distinction.

**Standing:** Working definition.

A difference between allowed descriptions that the declared registration or future-response family can distinguish. A distinction can survive only in a lift, receipt, relation, or history even when the projected endpoint is unchanged.

The term does not mean an independently additive bit unless an encoding and measure establish that interpretation.

**Sources:** [D6](#src-d6). Sections 4A, 6 and 9.

<a id="relational-complexity"></a>

### TH-012. Relational complexity

**Aliases:** Scalar complexity; Complexity of the thing.

**Standing:** Working definition.

A nonnegative functional measuring how much relational organization is present in a declared registered object:

\[
\mathcal C:\mathfrak R/\simeq_{\rm reg}\longrightarrow\mathbb R_{\geq0}.
\]

Equivalent registered presentations have equal complexity. A realization must specify its domain, what it measures, its unit, and its behavior under admitted changes. "The relational complexity of the thing" is the preferred reading.

No universal formula is supplied by vertex count, history length, winding, entropy, receipt capacity, or the exponent of a return operator alone.

**Sources:** [CHAT-COMPLEX](#src-chat-complex), [D6](#src-d6). Definition 4A.2; Appendix M.

<a id="complexity-increment"></a>

### TH-013. Complexity increment

**Standing:** Working definition.

The change of one fixed complexity functional across an admitted update on a consistent accounting domain:

\[
\Delta\mathcal C_i=\mathcal C(R_{i+1})-\mathcal C(R_i).
\]

It may be positive, zero, or negative. For a composable sequence it telescopes to the endpoint difference. Complete information preservation does not require every complexity increment to vanish, and \(c_{\rm Th}=1\) does not imply \(\Delta\mathcal C=1\).

**Sources:** [D6](#src-d6). Section 11A, equations RC3-RC6.

<a id="scalar-encoding"></a>

### TH-014. Scalar encoding

**Aliases:** Scalar; Multiplicative scalar; G^x.

**Standing:** Working definition.

An optional multiplicative representation of relational complexity:

\[
S_{\rm sc}(R)=G_{\rm enc}^{\mathcal C(R)},\qquad G_{\rm enc}>1.
\]

The inverse coordinate is \(\log_{G_{\rm enc}}S_{\rm sc}=\mathcal C\). The encoding base is fixed and dimensionless. It is neither a graph nor Newton's gravitational constant; the scalar is neither a wave amplitude nor mechanical action.

The sketch's \(G^x\) is normalized to this form. Treat \(x=\mathcal C(R)\) only after specifying the functional; a raw support count remains a special candidate statistic.

**Sources:** [CHAT-COMPLEX](#src-chat-complex), [D6](#src-d6). Definition 4A.3.

<a id="scalar-relay"></a>

### TH-015. Scalar relay

**Aliases:** Relational scalar relay.

**Standing:** Working definition.

Reconstruction of a scalar readout through continuity of relational accounting rather than necessarily transmitting a separate scalar label. Incremental relay obeys

\[
S_{{\rm sc},i+1}=S_{{\rm sc},i}G_{\rm enc}^{\Delta\mathcal C_i}.
\]

The receiver needs a known value or reference and sufficient locally available data to determine the increment, or sufficient output to compute the new value directly. Conservation in a joint output does not make information available at a receiver that does not possess it.

**Sources:** [D6](#src-d6). Definition 11A.1.

<a id="local-sufficiency"></a>

### TH-016. Local sufficiency

**Aliases:** Local increment criterion; Decoder sufficiency.

**Standing:** Finite mathematical criterion.

The condition that a required readout is determined by the data available to its decoder. If \(\eta(z)\) is the available data and \(\delta(z)\) the desired value, a decoder exists exactly when

\[
\eta(z)=\eta(z')\ \Longrightarrow\ \delta(z)=\delta(z').
\]

Thus identical accessible inputs requiring different updates refute that local interface. A sufficient increment decoder still needs an initial reference to reconstruct an absolute scalar.

**Sources:** [D6](#src-d6). Proposition 11A.3.

<a id="descriptive-orthogonality"></a>

### TH-017. Descriptive orthogonality

**Aliases:** Orthogonality in the scalar sketch.

**Standing:** Working definition.

Separation of descriptive roles: complexity asks how much registered organization is present, whereas phase and orientation describe aspects of how it is organized. The vertical bar in the motivating sketch was intended to indicate orthogonality in this sense.

This alone does not supply an inner product, statistical independence, freely combinable product coordinates, or a geometric right angle. Metric orthogonality must name its vector space and metric separately.

**Sources:** [CHAT-COMPLEX](#src-chat-complex), [D6](#src-d6). Section 13C.

## 02. Admissibility, action, and completed events

<a id="relation"></a>

### TH-018. Relation

**Aliases:** Available relation.

**Standing:** Working definition.

A locally available relational possibility between distinguishable states or transducers, represented where appropriate by adjacency or a typed interaction interface. A relation specifies what may interact with what; availability is not a realized transmission.

Graph adjacency and operational permission need not coincide: a declared admitted-input domain can further constrain the handoff.

**Sources:** [D6](#src-d6). Definition 4.1; Appendix A.

<a id="possibility"></a>

### TH-019. Possibility

**Aliases:** Relational possibility.

**Standing:** Working definition.

An alternative allowed by the declared relational and registration context before it is realized. Possibility describes available continuations, not an outcome frequency or a completed event.

The September 24 discussion explicitly separates possibility, probability, and event. Weighting an alternative and realizing it are additional operations or descriptions.

**Sources:** [CHAT-DS](#src-chat-ds), [CORE](#src-core), [D6](#src-d6).

<a id="continuation"></a>

### TH-020. Continuation

**Aliases:** Admissible continuation; Lawful continuation.

**Standing:** Working definition.

A lawful possible next relation or elementary operation from a specified present configuration and registration context. Write \(\operatorname{Adm}(z)\) for the admitted elementary continuations of complete context \(z\).

A continuation remains a possibility until realized. Counting continuations does not identify their weights or choose which one occurs.

**Sources:** [D6](#src-d6). Definition 5.1.

<a id="admissibility"></a>

### TH-021. Admissibility

**Aliases:** Permission; Lawful admission.

**Standing:** Working definition.

Satisfaction of the declared native relational constraints on an input, operation, history, or handoff. An operation's existence as a map or its desirable numerical output does not establish its admission.

In the QR program, law is sought in these permissions, constraints, and compositional invariants. An admission rule must be fixed independently of the result it is intended to explain.

**Sources:** [CHAT-DS](#src-chat-ds), [D6](#src-d6), [CORE](#src-core). Sections 4-7.

<a id="designation"></a>

### TH-022. Designation

**Aliases:** Outcome designation.

**Standing:** Working definition.

The identification or selection of a particular member of an admissible alternative set. A convention can designate names, while an execution or source law must account for an actual next outcome; these are different uses and must be stated.

The native lawful-pair geometry can determine two permitted continuations without preferring either one. Registration records a designation that occurred; it does not by itself produce the source law that chose it.

**Sources:** [CHAT-DS](#src-chat-ds), [CHAT-BORN](#src-chat-born), [D6](#src-d6).

<a id="informative-action"></a>

### TH-023. Informative action

**Aliases:** Realized continuation; Primitive informative action.

**Standing:** Working definition.

An admissible realized continuation that creates or changes a registered relational distinction. Informativeness is relative to the chosen registration or future-response family.

An action can be informative even when its projected endpoint returns, because a receipt or lifted relation changes. This use of "action" is not a dimensionful continuum action functional.

**Sources:** [D6](#src-d6). Definition 6.1.

<a id="informative-coupling"></a>

### TH-024. Informative coupling

**Aliases:** Informational coupling.

**Standing:** Working definition.

Dependence of admission or registered response on a retained variable after the comparison context and other variables are fixed. A witness is

\[
\operatorname{Adm}(z,r,s)\ne\operatorname{Adm}(z,r',s)
\]

or a different registered response to the same admitted test. Coupling specifies a usable dependence; action realizes a continuation. Mere coexistence or static correlation of labels is insufficient, and coupling can remain entirely local without an information current.

**Sources:** [D6](#src-d6). Definition 6A.1.

<a id="information-current"></a>

### TH-025. Information current

**Aliases:** Operational information current.

**Standing:** Working definition.

Transport of an operationally distinguishable payload through admitted field handoffs, with specified encoding, continuation, and downstream decoding. Each handoff must preserve downstream dependence on the incoming distinction within the complete-output account.

An information current uses informative coupling. The converse does not hold. Neither a nonzero parity nor the five-frame difference operator \(D_5\) alone establishes this operational current.

**Sources:** [D6](#src-d6). Definition 6A.2.

<a id="information-flux"></a>

### TH-026. Information flux

**Aliases:** Quantitative information flux.

**Standing:** Proposed quantitative object.

A quantitative account of operational information crossing a specified interface per a specified event-depth or time normalization. The payload distinction, measure, interface, and normalization must all be supplied before a numerical flux is defined.

This is a requirement for a future quantitative construction, not a universal formula or an identification with electric charge current or Poynting energy flux.

**Sources:** [D6](#src-d6). Section 6A.

<a id="admissible-transduction"></a>

### TH-027. Admissible transduction

**Aliases:** Information-preserving transduction.

**Standing:** Working definition.

A local transformation of a complete input that satisfies the declared constraints and remains reconstructible from its complete output:

\[
\mathcal T_e:\mathcal D_e\to\mathcal P_e\times\mathcal R_e,
\qquad \mathcal R^{\rm rec}_e\mathcal T_e=I_{\mathcal D_e}.
\]

In a finite classical description this is injectivity on the admitted domain. It need not be surjective onto every abstract output pair. Old memory is included in the recovered input when relevant.

**Sources:** [D6](#src-d6). Definition 7.1.

<a id="propagated-component"></a>

### TH-028. Propagated component

**Aliases:** Propagatable component; Transmitted component.

**Standing:** Working definition.

The output eligible to acquire an adjacent field address under an admitted handoff. It carries the continuing consequence, but need not alone determine the complete input.

An internal G60 coordinate is not a field address. Successive handoffs are different occurrences carrying the same specified relational content, not one completed event physically moving between sites.

**Sources:** [D6](#src-d6). Section 8.

<a id="trace"></a>

### TH-029. Trace

**Aliases:** Retained trace.

**Standing:** Working definition.

A retained relational consequence of informative action. A trace becomes a **receipt** when it serves the declared attestation or reconstruction account of the event.

Not every visible mark is sufficient for full recovery, and a trace's existence does not prove that a controller reads it or that it affects future admission.

**Sources:** [D6](#src-d6). Section 9.

<a id="receipt"></a>

### TH-030. Receipt

**Aliases:** Retained receipt; Event receipt.

**Standing:** Working definition.

A retained complementary distinction that, with the propagated component and relevant prior memory, accounts for a completed admitted transformation or handoff. Its precise interface must say whether it attests an occurrence, supports reconstruction, or both.

A receipt is part of the complete output, not discarded information. It can be constant when the propagated map is already sufficient. The full receipt need not be a copy of the payload.

**Sources:** [D6](#src-d6). Section 9; Appendix A.

<a id="attestation"></a>

### TH-031. Attestation

**Indexed component terms:** Attested actuality.

**Standing:** Working definition.

A record sufficient to establish that a specified occurrence happened under the declared witness interface. Attestation can be weaker than reconstructing its complete microscopic input.

The phrase "attested actuality" describes an event or history relative to its record; it is not a proof that every microscopic distinction is locally readable from that record.

Attested actuality is the occurrence as certified by that interface, rather than the attestation operation itself.

**Sources:** [D6](#src-d6). Section 9.

<a id="thalean-conservation-of-information"></a>

### TH-032. Thalean conservation of information

**Aliases:** Conservation of relational information; Event-local conservation; Complete-output conservation.

**Standing:** Working definition.

The requirement that an admitted complete transduction preserve the distinctions needed to recover its complete input. Classical recovery means injectivity; in the declared coherent representation the corresponding complete map is an isometry.

Conservation applies to propagated, retained, and correlated output jointly. It does not assert that \(\mathcal C\), entropy, energy, or freedom are all the same quantity, or that their independent scalar accounts add without correlations.

**Sources:** [D6](#src-d6). Section 11; Appendix A.

<a id="reconstruction"></a>

### TH-033. Reconstruction

**Indexed component terms:** Recoverability; Reconstruction map.

**Standing:** Finite mathematical definition.

Recovery of the complete admitted input from the image of the complete output map. A reconstruction map \(\mathcal R^{\rm rec}\) satisfies \(\mathcal R^{\rm rec}\mathcal T=I\) on the input domain.

This algebraic left inverse is not automatically an available reverse-time laboratory process. Recovery of a projected endpoint, an attestation record, and microscopic input recovery are different claims.

**Sources:** [D6](#src-d6). Proposition 11.1.

<a id="receipt-capacity"></a>

### TH-034. Receipt capacity

**Standing:** Finite mathematical criterion.

The number of receipt labels needed to complete a specified propagated map into an injective output. For a finite map \(p:X\to Y\), a receipt alphabet of size \(k\) suffices exactly when

\[
k\geq\max_{y\in Y}|p^{-1}(y)|.
\]

This counts the largest unresolved propagated-output fiber. It is not automatically a quantum cloning rule, a physical memory size, or the complexity of the entire object.

**Sources:** [D6](#src-d6). Section 11; Appendix B.

<a id="adjacent-handoff"></a>

### TH-035. Adjacent handoff

**Aliases:** Adjacent transmission; Carrier handoff.

**Standing:** Working definition.

The completed transfer of a propagated component across one admitted neighboring field relation while preserving the required complete accounting. It is the primitive field-propagation operation in the stated normalization.

A macro-action spanning several relationships does not become one primitive handoff by being assigned a single name.

**Sources:** [D6](#src-d6). Sections 8, 10 and 22.

<a id="event"></a>

### TH-036. Event

**Aliases:** Registered event.

**Standing:** Working definition.

An admitted realized relational occurrence with a distinguishable registered consequence and a place in a composition or causal dependency. This is the general occurrence sense used in the conversations.

For causal accounting, use the narrower **QR propagation event**. An internal comparison, an event-presentation symmetry, and an adjacent handoff are not automatically the same type of event. This distinction reconciles the overlapping event definitions in the core note.

**Sources:** [CORE](#src-core), [CHAT-DS](#src-chat-ds), [D6](#src-d6). CORE Event variants; D6 Section 10.

<a id="qr-propagation-event"></a>

### TH-037. QR propagation event

**Aliases:** Canonical QR event; Primitive propagation event.

**Standing:** Working definition.

One completed, receipted, admissible relational handoff: local transduction, an admitted adjacent address acquisition, and retained complementary accounting sufficient for the declared complete output.

Completion, not a proposed continuation or intermediate comparison, is the criterion. Historical placement comes from composition or causal dependency. Internal registration actions do not automatically acquire a new field address.

**Sources:** [D6](#src-d6). Definition 10.1.

<a id="event-primacy"></a>

### TH-038. Event primacy

**Standing:** Working definition.

The methodological principle that becoming is modeled through completed relational occurrences and their composition. States represent conditions before, after, or across occurrences; projected state succession alone need not specify the history.

This is an organizing principle of QR, not a claim that every conventional physical state description is invalid.

**Sources:** [D6](#src-d6). Section 10; Appendix A.

<a id="becoming"></a>

### TH-039. Becoming

**Aliases:** Event composition; Relational becoming.

**Standing:** Working definition.

The ordered composition of admitted realized events that transforms registered relational organization while preserving complete informational accounting. Write \(\gamma=e_n\circ\cdots\circ e_1\) when the successive domains match.

Composition supplies order before a duration in seconds is assigned. A complexity increment describes an aspect of becoming; it neither constitutes an event nor defines its occurrence order.

**Sources:** [D6](#src-d6). Section 12.

<a id="singular-actuality"></a>

### TH-040. Singular actuality

**Aliases:** Singular event interpretation.

**Indexed component terms:** Apparatus-conditioned admissibility.

**Standing:** Research interpretation.

The working interpretation that a realized occurrence is definite, while plurality belongs to the admissible alternatives and their represented relationships before registration. It does not imply that the finite carrier already contains a unique deterministic selector.

In the double-slit interpretation the complete apparatus conditions the admissible continuation structure. A which-path interaction changes that structure; this conceptual account does not substitute for an operational interference calculation.

**Sources:** [CHAT-DS](#src-chat-ds), [CHAT-BORN](#src-chat-born).

<a id="sigma"></a>

### TH-041. Sigma

**Aliases:** Relational encounter interface.

**Standing:** Working definition.

The relational comparison or encounter operation/interface in the terminology of the core note. Sigma names the comparison role, not the retained residue itself.

Its precise map, carrier, and admission contract must be supplied for each use. Do not merge it with a Pauli matrix, a sign coordinate \(\sigma_{t_*}\), or every permutation denoted \(\sigma\).

**Sources:** [CORE](#src-core). Retained receipt.

<a id="relational-spin-residue"></a>

### TH-042. Relational Spin Residue

**Aliases:** RSR.

**Standing:** Working definition.

The name reserved for a specifically established spin-related central or projective residue retained by an encounter. It is narrower than a generic trace or receipt.

A binary holonomy, an order-four group, or a central involution does not acquire physical spin meaning from its order alone. Sigma is the encounter role; RSR is the corresponding specifically justified residue.

**Sources:** [D6](#src-d6). Section 9; Appendix A.

<a id="law"></a>

### TH-043. Law

**Aliases:** Relational law; Effective law.

**Standing:** Working definition.

A stable constraint or invariant of admitted relational composition. In the current QR framing, relation is primitive to law: the question is what the declared relationships permit, not which external command is imposed on otherwise independent objects.

This framing does not make every admissible alternative actual or supply a unique selector. The requirement that a construction's law be specified independently of the target result remains unchanged.

**Sources:** [CORE](#src-core), [CHAT-DS](#src-chat-ds), [D6](#src-d6).

<a id="registration"></a>

### TH-044. Registration

**Aliases:** Historical registration; Event registration.

**Standing:** Working definition.

The admitted interaction or mapping that makes a realized distinction available as a record under a specified interface. It assigns an outcome or occurrence its retained representation and compositional address.

Registration is not synonymous with source selection, a universal collapse rule, or a complete microscopic copy. Faithful registration preserves the relevant output measure rather than repairing a deficient instrument.

**Sources:** [CORE](#src-core), [D6](#src-d6), [CHAT-DS](#src-chat-ds).

## 03. History, time, and causal accounting

<a id="registered-history"></a>

### TH-045. Registered history

**Aliases:** History; History as compositional record; Retained history.

**Standing:** Working definition.

The compositional record of realized events together with the retained distinctions needed by its declared interface. A literal event list is one representation, but a sufficient compression is permitted.

Endpoint permutation, parity, winding, predictive class, and full ordered word are different history readouts. A list of visible endpoints need not determine the registered history.

**Sources:** [D6](#src-d6). Section 13.

<a id="predictive-history-class"></a>

### TH-046. Predictive history class

**Aliases:** Predictive history; Future-response equivalence.

**Standing:** Working definition.

An equivalence class of histories with the same admitted future language and identical specified registered responses for every future test:

\[
h_1\sim h_2\iff \mathcal L(h_1)=\mathcal L(h_2)
\text{ and }O_{h_1}(w)=O_{h_2}(w)\text{ for every }w\in\mathcal L(h_1).
\]

Agreement only on the intersection of two different future languages is insufficient, especially if the intersection is empty. The equivalence is relative to the stated future-test family.

**Sources:** [D6](#src-d6). Section 15; Appendix A.

## 04. Freedom and the transport of opportunity

<a id="thalean-freedom"></a>

### TH-066. Thalean freedom

**Aliases:** Permissible freedom.

**Standing:** Working definition from September 24.

Permissible action remaining to a relational state in a specified constraint and registration context. In a declared linear model it is measured by the dimension of the admissible-action space, after identifying operationally equivalent directions.

Action may transform, preserve, create, or foreclose freedom. It need not consume every unchosen possibility. Complete information conservation constrains the accounting of what occurred, not preservation of every future opportunity.

<a id="permissible-action-space"></a>

### TH-067. Permissible-action space

**Aliases:** Local action space; Admissible-action space.

**Standing:** Working definition.

The set or structured space \(\mathcal P(z\mid\mathcal C_{\rm constr},\rho_*)\) of admissible action directions available from a state and context. A reference supplies coordinates; it does not itself manufacture an additional action degree.

Use "space" and its dimension only after specifying a linear, tangent, or other dimension-bearing structure. A bare finite alternative set has a cardinality, not a vector-space dimension by default.

**Sources:** [CHAT-FREEDOM](#src-chat-freedom).

<a id="local-freedom-degree"></a>

### TH-068. Local freedom degree

**Aliases:** Degrees of Thalean freedom; Local degrees of freedom.

**Standing:** Working definition.

The number of independent permissible-action directions in a declared dimension-bearing model:

\[
d_{\rm Th}(z)=\dim\mathcal P(z).
\]

If supplied action generators live in a vector space, use their span's rank, not the number of labels. Local freedom can increase after an event even while a fixed-frame completion count decreases.

**Sources:** [CHAT-FREEDOM](#src-chat-freedom).

<a id="history-compatible-completion-set"></a>

### TH-069. History-compatible completion set

**Aliases:** Fixed-frame completions; Omega_W(h).

**Standing:** Working definition.

For a fixed finite frame \(W\) with declared complete-history universe \(\Omega_W\), the completions consistent with observed history \(h\):

\[
\mathcal C_W(h)=\{\omega\in\Omega_W:h\preceq\omega\}.
\]

Appending an event restricts this same universe, so \(\mathcal C_W(he)\subseteq\mathcal C_W(h)\). Changing the horizon, apparatus, or universe invalidates an unqualified nested-set comparison.

**Sources:** [CHAT-FREEDOM](#src-chat-freedom).

<a id="residual-finite-frame-freedom"></a>

### TH-070. Residual finite-frame freedom

**Aliases:** Residual freedom; Finite-frame freedom.

**Standing:** Conditional finite-count definition.

A logarithmic count of history-compatible completions within one fixed finite universe:

\[
F_W(h)=\log_2|\mathcal C_W(h)|.
\]

The base is normalized here to two, consistently with the earlier explicit formula. On nonempty completion sets, extension cannot increase this quantity. An empty set is an incompatibility, not a zero-freedom state; \(\log_2 0\) is not a finite value.

This count is not automatically Shannon entropy or Thalean time.

**Sources:** [CHAT-FREEDOM](#src-chat-freedom).

<a id="resolved-freedom"></a>

### TH-071. Resolved freedom

**Aliases:** Resolved finite-frame freedom.

**Standing:** Conditional finite-count definition.

The reduction of residual completion freedom relative to a fixed starting history \(h_0\):

\[
R_W(h)=F_W(h_0)-F_W(h).
\]

It measures how many completion alternatives have been resolved in that fixed-frame counting model. It is not a universal amount of information gained, and it does not replace the oriented winding \(\tau_{\rm Th}\).

**Sources:** [CHAT-FREEDOM](#src-chat-freedom).

### TH-072. Carry-forward map

**Aliases:** Opportunity carry-forward; Freedom carry-forward.

**Standing:** Working definition.

A declared correspondence \(\kappa_e\) from action opportunities before an event to opportunities after it. It identifies which distinctions remain available, possibly in changed coordinates.

In a set model this can be a partial map. In a linear model specify its domain subspace, codomain, and rank. It is an opportunity map, not necessarily the complete information-preserving transduction \(\mathcal T_e\).

**Sources:** [CHAT-FREEDOM](#src-chat-freedom).

### TH-073. Freedom cost

**Aliases:** Opportunity cost of action.

The admissible freedom that an event fails to carry forward under a specified opportunity comparison. In the linear model,

\[
\operatorname{cost}_{\mathcal P}(e)=\dim\mathcal P(h)-\operatorname{rank}\kappa_e.
\]

For a partial linear map this counts both unavailable directions and distinctions merged by the map. In a set model use an explicitly chosen count or measure instead. Freedom cost is not erased microscopic information or an automatically priced economic loss.

**Sources:** [CHAT-FREEDOM](#src-chat-freedom).

<a id="costless-carried-freedom"></a>

### TH-074. Costless carried freedom

**Aliases:** Zero-cost possibility; Costless carry-forward.

**Standing:** Working definition.

Opportunity distinctions that survive an event without a loss under the declared carry-forward map. In the linear definition, zero freedom cost requires full rank relative to the pre-event opportunity space.

This does not mean the action consumes no energy, time, computation, or apparatus resource. It means that this particular opportunity account loses no independent directions.

**Indexed component terms:** Resolving action; Freedom-generating action; Exchange action; Costless action.

**Standing:** Working definition.

Descriptive classes relative to the chosen carry-forward comparison.
